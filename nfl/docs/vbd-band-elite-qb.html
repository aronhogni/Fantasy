Where in the draft does VBD actually win?

You drafted from seat 4, followed the app on every pick, and finished 4th of 10 — while the seat picking one slot behind you won. The suspicion was that value-over-replacement misranks the top of the board. It was measured. It does the opposite: almost the entire edge lives in rounds 1–3, and there is nothing left in the middle.

7 seasons 2019–2025 3 league shapes both of yours + the general one 2 metrics weekly wins and season points 50 cells paired, mirrored, in the same league

01The short answer

Taking Jonathan Taylor over Ja'Marr Chase at 1.4 was not a measurable mistake — and neither would the reverse have been.

Three separate claims were tested. Two are new results; one turned out to be already booked under a different name.

  • Does VBD misorder rounds 1–3? No — that is where its whole edge is. clears the bar
  • Is the RB-vs-WR ordering at the top right? Unresolved: not better, not worse than raw projection. null
  • Is an elite QB underpriced in a 1-QB league? No, in three independent forms. fails
  • Does maxPos.QB = 2 cost you a pick? Nothing measurable. null

The board is a function of the round — raw projection order inside the band, A-Ranking outside it — so exactly one variable moves: which ranking rule governs those picks. Everything else, including the room, is identical, and the two arms sit in the same league, mirrored across seats.

The bands do not sum to the whole, and that is measured rather than assumed. A different pick in round 2 changes who is on the board in round 9, so the interaction is real: +92.9 across bands against +36.6 for the whole draft in the 10-team shape (interaction −56.3), +103.2 against +136.2 in the 12-team half-PPR (+33.1). Both numbers are reported; neither is the other's error.

One band is significantly negative: in the general 12-team shape, using raw projection from round 8 onward beats VBD by 59.9 points. That is the deep-roster end, where the replacement-level argument should be strongest, and it is the one place the shipped rule loses.

The whole-draft row is a fifth cross-check, and it lands. The same comparison over the whole draft is already booked in h2h.json → q1 on five Sleeper seasons: +0.11 wins / +15.9 points in the 10-team PPR shape (null), +1.20 / +111.7 in the 12-team half-PPR (significant), +0.51 / +50.1 in the general shape (null). Seven seasons here read +0.20 / +36.6, +1.34 / +136.2, +0.60 / +55.5 — same signs, same significance pattern, same order of magnitude.

Which carries a consequence worth saying out loud, independent of any of the questions asked: the app's headline "VBD beats raw projection" was already null over the whole draft in your 10-team PPR league. What this decomposition adds is that the part of it which is real there is confined to the first three rounds — and that rounds 4 onward give some of it back (interaction −56.3).

03The mechanism — and why it isn't the question you asked

A +98-point edge in three picks looked too large for a ranking nuance. It is. Counting what each board actually takes with its first three picks, pooled over every seat and every season:

Same picture in both other shapes: raw projection spends 67% of its first three picks on quarterbacks; VBD spends 7–12%.

Rounds 1–3 is not where VBD ranks running backs above receivers correctly. It is where VBD refuses to draft quarterbacks.

Raw season projection puts quarterbacks on top, so ordering by it takes two of them in the first three picks — and only one can ever start. That effect is already booked, under a different name: forcing a QB in round 1 measures at −0.82 wins and −90.2 points in this same league shape (h2h.json → q2). The band result is largely a rediscovery of it through a different door.

So the question was asked again with quarterbacks removed from both arms. Both boards are forbidden from taking a QB in rounds 1–3, which leaves the RB/WR/TE ordering as the only variable. This is the actual Taylor-versus-Chase question:

All three intervals cross zero and the signs disagree across shapes. Conditional on not taking a quarterback early, the app's ordering of running backs and receivers at the top of the draft is not measurably better than sorting by raw projection — and not measurably worse. That is the honest answer to 1.4, and it is a null. Taylor over Chase was defensible; Chase over Taylor would have been equally defensible. Neither is the reason seat 5 won.

And an unasked question surfaced. Capping our own team at one TE is the largest positive arm anywhere in this run (+39.0 points, +0.32 wins) and it still does not clear the bar. The FLEX_SPLIT.TE = 0.193 sweep already stands on its own measurement (0 of 102 cells), so this is a flag, not a finding.

05Elite quarterback in a one-QB league

The argument is real on its face: the QB slot cannot be left empty, so the realised edge is Allen minus whichever quarterback you would otherwise have started, while VBD prices him against QB10 or QB12. Measured gap between the top projected QB and the shipped baseline: 51 to 91 points in the 10-team shape, 59 to 97 in the 12-team. The hypothesised magnitude is right. It does not survive being turned into a draft rule, in any of three forms.

You took Prescott at 10.7 and Lawrence at 11.4, and Lawrence never started. That reads like the clearest identifiable loss in the draft, and it isn't: forbidding the second quarterback costs 3.6 points and 0.03 wins in the Patriots shape, with the interval spanning [−39.8, +33.8]. All six cells contain zero.

The caveat runs in favour of the shipped rule, so it is worth stating plainly. The simulator has no waivers — rosters are frozen for the season — but the weekly lineup is chosen from who actually scored. A drafted backup therefore covers a bye week and an injury that a real manager would have covered for free off waivers, so the simulator flatters him. The true cost of that pick is somewhere between zero and small; what the measurement rules out is a large one. maxPos.QB = 2 is also a permission, not a recommendation: the board decides when a second quarterback is worth an eleventh-round pick, and the cap only allows it.

06What clears the bar, and what the gates were

Five conditions, all of them the repo's own: correct sign; season-clustered bootstrap excluding zero; player-clustered bootstrap excluding zero — the gate that gave vbdbase-lab 0 of 153; above a one-sided placebo ceiling on both mean and t; positive in a clear majority of seasons. And then it has to hold on both metrics, because wins and points track each other at rho 0.96–0.99 in the booked numbers, so a split reading is evidence about the instrument.

The one held-back cell fails on a technicality that deserves naming rather than hiding: its mean of +108.0 is four times the placebo mean of +26.9, but its t of 2.89 sits below a placebo t of 4.66. That ceiling t comes from a placebo with a tiny, very consistent effect — the exact pathology the repo already documented once, where a −1.5-point arm produced |t| = 22 because it lost every single season. The rule stands as written and the cell is reported as held back, not as passed.

Robustness on the source the app actually ships. Both arms read the same projection, so all seven seasons are usable — but Sleeper only covers five, and "not significant in the source the app uses" is a real objection in this repo. On the five Sleeper seasons alone, the rounds-1–3 band still excludes zero in all three shapes: +71.4 [+19.6, +129.4], +75.7 [+2.7, +157.2], +69.4 [+8.2, +123.9] points.

Four gates ran before any number was allowed outeach one can kill the run

  • Self-test. The board against itself must give exactly 0 wins and 0 points. It does.
  • Identity. The placebo at weight 0 must be bit-identical to the A-Ranking board, key for key. It is.
  • Anchors at both ends. An oracle board must win big (+4.21 wins) and reversed ADP must lose big (−9.71).
  • Cross-anchor. The world here is a verbatim copy of h2h-lab's, so A-Ranking versus ADP is re-run and compared against the booked figure: +2.71 wins against +2.58 booked, +2.70 against +2.52. Out of family, the run dies rather than reporting. The whole-draft rows agree with the booked q1 → proj numbers too, though that comparison is a check rather than a gate.

07What wiring would involve — and why nothing is wired

Nothing was changed. src/ is untouched. The measurement is a new lab and one output file.

Nothing here asks to be wired, and that is the useful part. The one result that clears the bar says the shipped rule is already right where it was doubted — a confirmation needs no code. The elite-QB family fails, so wiring it would be wiring noise. The second-QB cap is null, so removing maxPos.QB = 2 would trade a measured zero for an unmeasured one.

Two things are genuinely open, and both are new questions rather than pending changes:

  • Rounds 8+ in the general shape lose 59.9 points. Only one of three shapes, and not one of yours, so it is a lead and not a defect. If it repeats in your shapes it would argue for a shallower baseline late in the draft — which is dynamic VBD in reverse, and dynamic VBD is already measured and failed (−89, −31, −97, +12).
  • 58% of VBD's first three picks are tight ends in your PPR league. The TE cap is the largest positive arm in this run and still fails the bar, so there is nothing to change today. It is the most interesting number I found that I was not asked about.

Sample honesty: one mock draft is one draft, and it did not drive any conclusion here — it only chose which questions to ask. The measurements run over 7 seasons and 3 shapes with 5,000+ simulated leagues per cell, and 46 of 50 cells came back null. The season starts in September; there is time to take the tight-end question apart properly.
